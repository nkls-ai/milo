# What Milo does, in detail

The full list, with the settings behind each feature. The [README](../README.md) has the short version with pictures. Settings live in `~/Milo/config.toml`; the Settings window changes the common ones.

- **Dictation (Wispr Flow replacement).** Hold `fn`, speak, release. Whisper large-v3-turbo transcribes on the GPU while you speak, phrase by phrase, so the text is pasted into the focused app right after you let go, however long you talked. Filler words are dropped. Your clipboard is restored afterwards. For hands-free, double-tap `fn`: a lock shows next to Milo, tap `fn` once more to paste, `esc` to throw it away. A hands-free recording stops by itself after 5 minutes (`[ui] hands_free_max_s`).
- **Learns your words.** When you fix a word Milo pasted (he wrote "Cloud Code", you make it "Claude Code"), he notices within 45 s and spells it your way next time: names after one fix, other words after two. The fixes and the names you add yourself are in `~/Milo/dictionary.md`, a plain list you can edit (menu bar › Dictation words…). Switch it off in Settings › Dictation (`[dictation] learn`). Password fields are never read, and only the fixed words reach the brain, never the rest of the field.
- **Live words.** While you speak, for dictation and when talking to Milo, the words appear in a small pill next to Milo. `[dictation] stream` (or menu bar › Dictation style) picks how: `"preview"` (default) shows them and pastes on release, `"type"` also types each phrase into the app at the pause after it, `"off"` shows nothing and transcribes the whole recording on release.
- **Talk to Milo.** Hold right `⌥`, speak, release. Double-tap it for hands-free, like dictation. Your words show while you speak, and Milo gets them the moment you let go. He thinks with a fast chat model (Claude Haiku through the `claude` CLI by default, or any OpenAI-style API with `[chat] use = "openai_compat"`), answers in a speech bubble, and speaks with Kyutai's Pocket TTS on your Mac's CPU, sentence by sentence: about 7x faster than real time on an M3, in English and German. OmniVoice (a cloned voice on the GPU, slower), ElevenLabs (cloud) and the Mac's `say` are the alternatives under `[tts]`. Press the key while he talks and he goes quiet at once; let go without saying anything and he picks up where he stopped. If his first word is slow in coming he says a short "Hm." first (`[chat] backchannels`). To talk over him without the key, try `[chat] voice_barge_in = true` (experimental: it hasn't been tested with real speakers yet).
- **Milo by message.** Write to Milo in Telegram from your phone and he works on your Mac: finds and sends you a file (after your yes), answers from your calendar, starts helpers and tells you when they're done. Risky steps ask you in the chat. Pairing takes a code shown on the Mac, no port is opened, and only your chat counts. Off by default; see [docs/setup/messages.md](setup/messages.md). iMessage works too, experimentally.
- **Milo, the wren.** Milo is a small lavender wren with a marigold scarf. He blinks, follows your cursor, leans in and nods while you talk, ruffles his tuft while thinking and sleeps tucked in at night. When he works on something on screen he flies there and points at it with a wing. When nothing is going on he flies off the screen after 15 seconds and swoops back when you hold a key.
- **Settings.** "Settings…" in the menu bar item (or the gear in the Milo window) picks how often he tells you what he's doing (`[chat] updates`), how dictation shows your words, his keys and whether he speaks his answers (`[ui] voice`). Changes are saved in `~/Milo/config.toml` with your comments kept, and apply at once. The menu bar's "Dictation style" changes the same setting as the window.
- **The Milo window.** Click Milo (or "Open conversation" in the menu bar) to read the conversation, type to him, and watch what he does when he works on something for you: "Looking in your Downloads folder", "Editing notes.md". Click a step to see the exact command. The speaker button decides whether he reads typed answers aloud. The history is kept in `~/Milo/state/transcript.jsonl`.
- **Helpers.** Ask for something that takes work and Milo hands it to a helper, then keeps talking with you. Several helpers work at once (`[harness] max_helpers`, 3 by default), and work on the screen goes one at a time. A small bird next to Milo stands for each helper, with what it is doing; click it to see its steps in the Milo window, where Stop ends it. Ask "how far are you?", change a task while it runs ("make it 2000 instead"), or say "stop that one" or "stop everything". Each helper gets only the tools its task needs, so a calendar question is answered in a few seconds, and simple reads run on a lighter model (`[harness.codex] light_model`). When you asked by voice and the work takes a while, Milo says one short line after about 8 s ("Opening Excel now…"), then waits for the result. `[chat] updates` decides how much Milo tells you along the way: `"quiet"`, `"bubble"` (short notes, the default) or `"chatty"` (he says it). It applies without a restart.
- **Your apps.** Milo can look in your calendar and reminders, add to them, search and read your mail, write drafts (he never sends mail; you press Send), search, read and add to notes, run your shortcuts and find documents in your home folder, including PDFs, Word files and spreadsheets. Each one can be switched off under `[connections]` in `~/Milo/config.toml`.
- **Your home and Focus.** "Turn off the kitchen lights", „Heizung auf 21 Grad“, "movie night": Milo switches lights, plugs, scenes, heating, fans and blinds at once, with a card and Undo back to how they were. Locks, garage doors and the alarm ask first, every time (`[approvals.levels] lock`). He uses Home Assistant when `[home.home_assistant]` has its address (a long-lived token in `~/Milo/secrets`), else your Apple Home scenes and devices through your shortcuts in a "Milo Home" folder (`[home] use`). "Do Not Disturb on" or "Focus Work off" just happens too; for Do Not Disturb Milo makes the two shortcuts it needs himself and you click Add once. Setup: [docs/setup/home.md](setup/home.md).
- **Texts and calls.** "Text Anna I'm running ten minutes late", "call Mum", "ruf Papa per Video an": Milo finds the person in your contacts (a first name, a nickname, or "Mum" and "Papa" through the family on your own card), says which number he'll use and the whole message, and asks every time, in every mode and from your phone too. Then Messages sends it (iMessage, or a text through your iPhone), or FaceTime starts the call or rings through your iPhone. Two Annas get a question: "Which Anna: Anna Berg or Anna Kim?". A number you say works without contacts. Nothing takes a message or a call back, so the card has no Undo. macOS asks once for Contacts and once for controlling Messages; Milo tells you before. Switch them off with `contacts`, `messages` and `calls` under `[connections]` ([ADR 39](adr/0039-messages-and-calls.md)).
- **He asks first.** Reading and drafting just happen. Anything that goes to another person, deletes, buys or runs a shortcut waits for a yes: Milo asks in the bubble and the window with Yes and No buttons, and you can also hold the talk key and say "yes" or "no". The question stays open until you answer, and Milo asks once more if you didn't; a helper waiting for your answer goes on after a yes. Passwords, money transfers and security settings he never does. Change the levels under `[approvals.levels]`.
- **Memory.** Milo remembers what happened and what he learned about you, for years, and brings the bits that fit into each conversation. It all stays in `~/Milo/memory/` as plain files. "What Milo knows…" in the menu bar lists it, with Edit and Forget; `profile.md` shows it too, and your edits there count. At night, while you're away and the Mac is plugged in, he tidies up the day in one model call.
- **Milo looks when you ask.** "Look at my screen, what do you think?" Milo reads the window in front and takes a picture of it, then answers. An eye shows next to him while he looks. He never looks on his own.
- **What's this?** Select something and press `⌃⌥M` (or pick "What's this?" in the menu bar item). Milo explains the selection, or the whole window if nothing is selected. Change the shortcut with `[ui] whats_this_key`.
- **Milo uses the Mac for you.** "Put these numbers into the Excel sheet", "find the email from Anna and file it". The worker gets tools to read windows, press buttons, type and click. He works in the background wherever he can — pressing buttons, filling in fields and scrolling without touching your mouse or keyboard — so you can keep working on the side. His own pointer, in his colours, shows you where he's working, the window he's in gets a soft outline that says "Milo is working here", and Pip perches on its corner. Press `esc` or the Stop button to stop him. When something can only be done the old way (typing into an Excel cell, clicking a web page), he asks "Can I borrow your mouse for a moment?" first, uses it, and puts your pointer back — and while he's borrowing it, any touch stops him. A new table in Excel or Numbers, or a new text document in TextEdit or Pages, is one step that needs no question. Each action shows up as a step in the Milo window. Before he sends, deletes, buys, runs a script or replaces filled cells, he asks in the bubble, in one short sentence. A question stays open until you answer it; he asks once more after 20 seconds, and a helper waits for your answer ([ADR 31](adr/0031-talk-as-naturally-as-dictation.md)). A question about work in an app (replacing cells, a script for that app, borrowing the mouse there) has three buttons: Allow covers the rest of that task in that app, Allow always stops asking about that app, No. Settings › "How often Milo asks before he acts" picks Every step, Once per task or Don't ask (auto, the default), where what you asked for needs no question; sending, deleting for good, buying and money still ask ([ADR 23](adr/0023-stop-for-what-cant-be-undone.md)). The always-allowed apps are listed there with Remove ([ADR 22](adr/0022-one-question-per-piece-of-work.md)). He never touches password fields or password apps.
- **Opening things.** "Open Chrome and go to YouTube", "open my Downloads folder": Milo says "Opening YouTube in Chrome" and it opens while he says it, in front, with no helper and no question. A link that came from a mail or a web page asks first. Keys and clicks a helper sends always go to the app the task is about, never to whatever happens to be in front, and never to Milo himself ([ADR 28](adr/0028-open-at-once-and-answer-in-the-users-language.md)). He answers in the language you just spoke or typed in, whatever language his memories are in.
- **Music, volume and brightness.** "Play Taylor Swift", "play my running playlist", "play some jazz", "volume to 40", "louder", "mute", "make the screen brighter": it just happens, with one short line ("Playing Taylor Swift.", "Volume at 40.") and a card with Undo, which pauses again, sets the volume back or dims the screen again. Music plays from your Apple Music library, in the app you name or else the one already playing (`[connections] music_app` sets a default); something not in the library opens Music's search, and Spotify, which can't play a search without its web API, opens its search and says so. "What's this song?" is answered from what plays, without a helper. Each can be switched off (`[connections] music`, `volume`, `brightness`) ([ADR 36](adr/0036-music-volume-brightness.md)).
- **Writes where you are.** Click into a reply, hold right `⌥` and say "answer Anna, Sunday works but make it one". Milo looks at the email in front of you, writes the answer and pastes it straight into the field you were in, in one piece, about 0.1 s after the model finishes. He says "It's in Mail, have a look." Nothing is sent: you read it and press Send. "Undo that" takes it out again if the field hasn't changed since (else he tells you to press ⌘Z). He never pastes into a password field, a terminal, his own window, or a field you've moved away from; then the text waits in the Milo window with Copy. Text he wrote after reading something someone else wrote goes in only when your own words asked for writing ([ADR 33](adr/0033-milo-writes-where-you-are.md)).
- **Undo, now or later.** Every quick action and every task can be undone: Undo on the card, or "undo that". Timers, to-dos and Milo's own reminders can also be taken back later by name ("cancel the pasta timer"). Files and workbooks are copied before a helper changes them ([ADR 26](adr/0026-undo-what-a-task-did.md)).
- **Routines.** After Milo did something, say "remember how to do this, call it the Monday report". A helper writes the steps down as a plain recipe, with the things that change each time (the month, a file name) as questions, and shows it in the Milo window: Save, or say what to change. Then "do the Monday report", "the Monday report for October", or "every Monday at 9". He follows the recipe, checks each step, and asks when something looks different. The recipes are Markdown files in `~/Milo/routines/` you can edit. The window's Routines section has Run now, pause and delete. `[routines]` caps scheduled runs a day and sets how late a missed run may still run.
- **Meeting notes.** When Zoom, Teams, FaceTime, Webex, a Slack huddle, a Discord, WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or Skype call, or Google Meet in a browser uses your microphone, Milo asks once in the bubble: "Looks like a meeting. Should I take notes?", with Take notes, Not this time, "Always for Zoom" and "Never for Zoom". The last two are remembered (Settings, Meeting notes, where you can undo them). While he records he holds a notepad and writes, and a capsule next to him says "Taking notes · 12:34" with Pause and Stop; the menu bar shows the time too. A click on the capsule opens the meeting in the Milo window: who spoke, the transcript as it comes in (about a minute behind), a button that copies a line for the call's chat to tell the others, and a field to ask about the meeting ("What did I miss?"). For anything he doesn't notice, ⌃⌥N (`[ui] meeting_key`) or "Take meeting notes" in the menu starts and stops it by hand. Tell him "that was Anna" and he remembers her voice for next time. After Stop he says what he's writing, then "Your notes are ready" with an Open button. The page shows the notes, the to-dos with "Add to Reminders" on each, and the transcript; the files are in `~/Milo/meetings/<date-title>/` (`notes.md`, `transcript.md`), one "Show in Finder" away. Everything is transcribed on your Mac, the audio is deleted once the notes are written (`[meetings] keep_audio`), and if the brain or the app crashes mid-call, nothing already recorded is lost. `[meetings] auto = "calendar"` takes notes without asking during meetings in your calendar; `"off"` never asks. Recording others needs their consent in many places, Germany among them: that is what the "Copy for the chat" button is for.
- **Weather.** "Do I need an umbrella tomorrow?", "how warm is it on Saturday?": Milo answers at once from a short forecast for where you are (today and tomorrow with the hours it rains, then the rest of the week). It comes from Open-Meteo (free, no account), read at most every 45 minutes. The place is your time zone's city unless you set `[weather] place`; that name is all that goes to Open-Meteo. `[weather] units = "imperial"` for °F and mph, `enabled = false` turns it off.
- **Alarms.** "Wake me at 7", "set an alarm for 6:30 tomorrow", "cancel my alarm". Milo rings with a chime and his voice, again and again for up to 4 minutes, until you press a Milo key or Stop, type to him, or say "stop" or "okay, I'm up". "Five more minutes" snoozes it. Alarms are kept in `~/Milo/state/alarms.json`, so they ring after a restart, and each gets a card with Undo. Up to a week ahead. Milo can't ring while the computer sleeps (waking it needs admin rights), so keep it awake and plugged in overnight; he tells you once, and says so when he missed one.
- **Reminders and nudges cost no tokens.** Say "remind me at 9 to call the dentist" and Milo writes a line to `~/Milo/reminders.md` (a helper asks you first). The local heartbeat fires it later without calling the LLM. Morning greetings, break nudges and "welcome back" are local rules too. Cap: `llm_calls_per_day = 0`.

## Settings and the worker

Settings live in `~/Milo/config.toml`: chat model, harness, voice, keys, heartbeat rules. The Settings window changes the common ones for you; after editing the file by hand, restart the brain from the menu bar item. Milo never overwrites `~/Milo/AGENTS.md` or `~/Milo/config.toml`, so a workspace from before gets the advice for the user's apps and for using the Mac only if you copy the "The user's apps" and "Using the Mac" sections from `brain/src/milo/workspace_template/AGENTS.md`. The tools carry the most important rules themselves.

The worker runs with Milo's tools only: Codex gets a home of its own in `~/Milo/.codex` (sharing only your login), and Claude Code skips your user settings, skills and MCP servers, so your own setup doesn't reach it. Helpers work in `~/Milo/work` and can't change Milo's own files: settings, persona, instructions, reminders, routines, memory and keys ([ADR 20](adr/0020-helpers-never-write-milos-control-files.md)). `[hands] enabled = false` takes the Mac tools away from the worker, and `applescript = false` turns off scripts only. Jobs on the screen take a while, so give the harness more time: `[harness.codex] timeout_s = 600`. Questions before a risky step wait `[approvals] timeout_s`; an old `[hands] confirm_timeout_s` is ignored with a warning in the log.
