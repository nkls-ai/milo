# Milo, your coworker

Written 2026-10-30. How Milo presents himself, and how he makes money. [VISION.md](VISION.md) has the features, [CHARACTER.md](CHARACTER.md) the personality.

## The idea

Milo isn't "an AI" or a chatbot. He's a coworker who lives on your Mac. You tell him things the way you'd tell a colleague, and he gets them done, remembers them, and follows up.

A good coworker:

- knows your stuff: your projects, your people, what happened yesterday,
- does the boring parts without being asked twice,
- asks before anything risky, and says plainly when he couldn't do something,
- follows up ("Tom still hasn't answered, should I nudge him?"),
- keeps working while you're away, and you can reach him from your phone,
- knows when to be quiet.

The homepage in [site/](../site/README.md) puts this into a page: "Just tell Milo.", the wren, and a demo for each thing he does.

## How he looks and sounds

- **The character.** A small figure in the corner with a real personality. Skins (Pip the bird, a little dragon, Kit, Glow) let people make him theirs. People share characters they like, the way Duolingo's owl gets shared.
- **Helpers.** When Milo works on several things, small versions of him carry each task. Parallel work becomes something a non-technical person understands at a glance.
- **Milo's cursor.** On screen he has his own pointer in his colour, so you always see where he's working and your mouse stays yours.
- **How he talks.** Short, dry, warm. "On it." "Done, it's in your Downloads." "That one looks like a scam, don't click it." Never sugary, never "Great question!".

## Who it's for

Everyone builds AI products for people who already use AI. Milo is for the rest: parents, small business owners, people in practices and offices who have heard of ChatGPT and don't use it. The test is whether the user's mother can install him and get help in the first five minutes.

Trust is the product for them. Milo asks before anything that can't be undone, shows every step, works locally where he can, keeps copies so "undo that" works, and stops the moment you touch the mouse or press esc.

Families are the way in: a son or daughter sets Milo up for their parents, and Milo can ask them for help when he's stuck.

## Pricing (draft)

| | Free | Milo Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Dictation | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Talking to Milo | About 20 conversations a day | Unlimited |
| Memory, reminders, calendar | Yes | Yes |
| Doing work on the computer | A few small tasks a week, so people see it | Unlimited, several helpers at once |
| Meeting notes | No | Yes |
| Milo by message (WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage) | No | Yes |
| Memory synced across devices | No | Yes |

Free is cheap to run: dictation and the voice are local, and short chats use a fast model. People get used to talking to Milo before they pay. A family plan and a small-office plan come later. Prices to test: around €15 to €20 a month for Plus.

## What decides whether this works

1. Computer use works reliably on 30 everyday tasks written the way a non-technical person would say them.
2. People still use Milo every day after the first week.
3. A stranger can install him without help (signing, notarization, onboarding).
4. The hosted backend costs less than Plus brings in.

## Competition, honestly

Apple is giving Siri screen awareness, OpenAI and Anthropic ship computer use, and specialists cover single jobs: Granola for meeting notes, Wispr Flow for dictation, Raycast for shortcuts, Clawdbot for an assistant you message. Milo won't win on feature count. He can win on the audience they ignore, on trust, on a character people like, and on families bringing in families.
