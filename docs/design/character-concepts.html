Bodies for Milo

Same Milo in each: the voice, the manners and the life described in CHARACTER.md. Only the drawing changes. Each one sits at real size in the corner of a desktop, on a light and a dark wallpaper. Move your mouse and they watch it.

Update, 30 October, later: five looks are now built in the app and picked in Settings: Pip, the robot, a new round dragon, Kit and Glow. See Built in the app below; the live sketches after it are the original directions.

Update, 30 October: Pip is now drawn in the app and is Milo's default look. The robot stays available with [ui] character = "classic". Kit, Glow and Puff further down are three new directions, in case Pip isn't the one.

Built in the app

Rendered from the Swift code (MILO_RENDER_DIR=… swift test --filter SkinRender). Every look gets the same signals, so each blinks, listens, thinks, speaks, sleeps, flies and points, and each brings its own colours and springs to the bubble and the windows. [ui] character = pip, classic, dragon, kit or glow.

Visor

The idea
Today's purple robot, grown up a little. His face is a dark glass panel where the eyes glow, the antenna light shows what he's doing, and two floating hands let him point.
Why it stands out
It keeps what already works and fixes the blankness: the glowing face reads from across the desk, and hands give him gestures a blob can't make.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Low. Today's shapes plus a visor and two circles for hands. The glow is one blurred copy drawn once. Two or three days.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
Friendly, but a robot says "gadget", and the glowing antenna is a toy detail.

Pip

The idea
A small round bird who lives in the corner, with a tuft on his head where the antenna was. He cocks his head to listen, flies to whatever he's working on and points with a wing.
Why it stands out
Nobody's tech mascot is a garden bird. Head tilts, hops and short flights are what real birds do, so the life layer looks natural instead of programmed.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Medium. Eight simple shapes, but the wing flap and head tilt need tuning to feel right. The flap only runs while he flies, so idle cost stays the same. About a week.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
The safest bet. Calm colours, small eyes, no cartoon grin. He reads as a bird at the window, not a toy or a gadget.

Pointer

The idea
The Mac's arrow pointer, come alive. His tip is his nose and his finger. When he works on something he flies there and taps it, a click you can watch.
Why it stands out
No other assistant looks like this, and pointing in Phase 2 is his whole body. The thinking state winks at the spinning wait wheel.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Low to draw: one path and a face. The real cost is rules so he's never mistaken for the real pointer: always bigger, always with a face, never moving while you do.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
Clever if you think about computers. If you already lose the pointer on screen, a second one is a problem.

Kit new

The idea
A fox folded from warm paper, all flat facets and crisp creases. To go somewhere he folds himself into a paper plane, glides over, lands and unfolds to point with a paw.
Why it stands out
Nobody's assistant is made of paper. He looks handmade and grown-up, like a good greeting card, and folding into a plane makes every flight a small show without being silly.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Medium to high. The facets are a dozen flat polygons and cheap to draw. The fold between fox and plane needs a morph between two shapes with the same number of corners. About a week and a half.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
Paper and foxes are both familiar and friendly. A few people find foxes sly; the round eyes and cream cheeks take care of most of that.

Glow new

The idea
A small warm light with paper-thin wings, somewhere between a firefly and a lantern. He glows brighter while you talk, dims to an ember at night, and points by shining a little spotlight on the thing he means.
Why it stands out
A spotlight is the clearest way to say "this one" on a busy screen, clearer than a wing or a paw. A soft light in the corner is also pleasant to live with in the evening.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Low to medium. A halo blurred once, an orb and a face. His wings beat only while he flies, so sitting still costs what Milo costs today.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
Lovely in the evening, but anything with wings and feelers can read as a bug. Leaning toward the lantern side, with no legs and no body segments, fixes most of that.

Puff new

The idea
A small cloud with a face. His weather is his mood: a sun peeks out behind him when something worked, a moon when he sleeps, little cloudlets drift up while he thinks. He points with a wisp.
Why it stands out
There's no animal to like or dislike, and everyone reads weather at a glance. Floating is what clouds do, so flying across the screen needs no explaining.
What it costs in SwiftUI
Low. Five circles, a face, and a sun and a moon behind him. A day or two more than Visor.
Would a 60-year-old like him?
Gentle and calm. The risk is a nursery mobile; a small face, misty colours and no rainbow keep him grown-up.

My pick: Pip

The person Milo is for has never used an assistant and is a little wary of computers that talk. A bird is the one shape here she already likes before he says a word. It also does the Phase 2 job best: flying to a spot and pointing with a wing is something a bird does anyway, so "Milo flew over to the spreadsheet" sounds charming instead of strange.

Visor is the fallback if the purple robot has fans already: cheapest to build and least risky. Pointer is the boldest idea, but it fails the main audience.

Of the three new ones, Kit is my second choice after Pip: just as warm, with more design in him, and the paper plane is a flight people will remember. Puff is the safest of all for strangers. Glow has the best way to point, and the spotlight could be borrowed by any of the others.
